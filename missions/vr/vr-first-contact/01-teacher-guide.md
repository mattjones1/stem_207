# VR First Contact: instructor guide

**Workshop 1 of the VR / AR series · Grades 5–8 · 90 minutes · 10 Meta Quest 3s · 9 students**
Workshop plan by Luke Peelen for STEM Coliseum (2025–2026), adapted for STEM 207.

## Learning objectives
Students will:
- Set up the boundary (Guardian) safely and on their own.
- Move around the Meta Quest 3 home environment and menus.
- Explain the difference between VR and AR modes on the Quest 3.
- Try at least two kinds of VR: 360° video and an interactive 3D place.
- Describe what VR is in their own words, and how the headset tracks movement.
- Name one thing VR can do that a screen can't.

## Safety protocols: required before every session

**For every workshop**
- **Boundary:** every student sets up the boundary before the headset goes on. No exceptions. Check that each one is at least 6 × 6 feet of clear space.
- **Hygiene:** wipe every headset with a microfiber cloth and a lens wipe between students. Use washable silicone face covers, and replace foam inserts if they're shared.
- **Motion sickness:** warn students first that mild dizziness is normal. If a student feels sick, dizzy, or uncomfortable, the headset comes off at once, with no questions. Never pressure a student to continue.
- **Time limits:** no more than 30 minutes in a headset in a row. Build in a 5-minute break for longer activities. Students with known motion sensitivity start with 10–15 minutes.
- **Walking:** headsets come off before anyone walks. Seated or standing-in-place activities are best for beginners.
- **Controllers:** wrist straps on every time. Clear chairs, bags, and obstacles before headsets go on.
- **Content:** parental supervision on, content limited to E / E10+ for grades 5–8. Check before every session, because updates can reset settings.
- **Stop word:** "PAUSE." Anyone can call it at any time. Respond right away.

**For this workshop**
- It's most students' first time in VR. Say clearly that feeling a bit disoriented is normal, and that stopping early is fine.
- Younger students: 15 minutes at most per session, stationary experiences only (360 video, no moving around in the world).
- No student uses a headset another student wore until it has been cleaned.

## Apps

| App | Cost | Why it's used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meta Quest home | Free (built in) | Home navigation, avatars, and menus |
| YouTube VR | Free | 360° video: a calm first VR moment with low motion-sickness risk |
| Wander | Plan says free (basic); store listing shows $9.99. **Check.** | Visit almost anywhere on Earth in street view |
| Nature Treks VR | About $5. **Check.** | Calm places with little movement; the backup for students who feel dizzy |

Install and test every app on every headset before the workshop. Open each one once so it's signed in and has no updates waiting.

## Setup checklist
- [ ] 10 headsets fully charged (green light on the charger). A Quest 3 needs 2+ hours.
- [ ] All apps installed and opened at least once
- [ ] Parental controls and content limits checked
- [ ] Play spaces taped on the floor
- [ ] Silicone face covers on every headset
- [ ] Microfiber cloths and lens wipes stocked
- [ ] Casting to a TV or monitor working (if you're using it)
- [ ] A pre-approved YouTube VR playlist set up
- [ ] Scavenger hunt cards and exit tickets printed
- [ ] Headsets in sleep mode, lined up for handing out
- [ ] Stop word posted where everyone can see it

**STEM 207 privacy rule:** students never sign in. Headsets use the program's own accounts. Avatars and screenshots carry no real names, and screenshots stay in VR (Wander), not of classmates in passthrough.

## 90-minute plan

| Time | Activity | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | Welcome and hook | Ask: "Has anyone ever been somewhere you will never be able to go in real life?" Show a 30-second clip on a regular screen of what's coming. |
| 0:10–0:20 | Safety briefing | Every rule, a boundary setup demo, the stop word, and the signs of motion sickness. Students set up their play spaces. |
| 0:20–0:30 | Boundary and avatar | Students power on one at a time. Help each one set and check the boundary. Students make their avatar. |
| 0:30–0:45 | YouTube VR | Three 360° videos: an underwater dive, a space station view, and a student choice from the approved playlist. Check comfort as you circulate. |
| 0:45–0:55 | Wander | Each student says one place they've always wanted to see, then visits it. Watch for motion sickness. |
| 0:55–1:05 | Break and discussion | Headsets off, 5-minute break. What surprised you? What felt real? What felt fake? Could you tell turning your head from turning your whole body? |
| 1:05–1:15 | AR mode demo | Passthrough: virtual objects in the real room. Students take turns. What's the difference between VR and AR? |
| 1:15–1:25 | VR scavenger hunt | Find the 5 landmarks on the card in Wander and take a headset screenshot of each. |
| 1:25–1:35 | Wrap-up | Exit ticket: 3 words about the experience. Share out. Preview Workshop 2: "Next time you will BUILD your own virtual world." Clean and store headsets. |

Times are approximate. Never rush the safety briefing or the first headset session.

## Instructor notes
- Open every app before students arrive. Opening menus mid-session kills momentum.
- Watch every student for their first 5 minutes in a headset. Most motion sickness happens in the first session.
- The scavenger hunt is the most energizing part. Make sure Wander loads on every headset first.
- Students often make their boundaries too small. Check each one.
- Save the AR demo until students have had 20+ minutes in VR, so the contrast is clear.
- A student who feels dizzy in YouTube VR moves to a stationary experience (Nature Treks VR).
- Collect the exit tickets. They show comfort levels before Workshop 2.

**Misconceptions to listen for**
- "VR and AR are the same." In VR the real room disappears; in AR it stays, with virtual things added.
- "It's just a screen on your face." The headset tracks your head and hands, which is why turning your body changes the view.

**Question to ask afterward:** "What felt real, and what gave it away as fake?"

## Motion sickness quick response
1. **Watch for:** a student who takes the headset off suddenly, goes quiet, looks pale, holds their stomach, or says they feel dizzy or hot.
2. **Right away:** sit them in a chair (not on the floor). Take the headset off gently. Don't ask them to do anything yet.
3. **Recovery:** look at a fixed point across the room (not a screen). Offer water. Cool air helps: a window or a fan.
4. **Going back in:** only if the student wants to and feels completely better. Stationary 360 video only, no moving around.
5. **Write it down:** which student (display name or initials), which app, and about how long. Keep it with program staff.
6. **Prevention:** stationary first, moving later. Short sessions (15 minutes) before long ones. Students who just ate are more likely to feel sick.
