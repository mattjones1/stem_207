# Market research: coding for grades 2–8 (October 28, 2026)

Gathered for planning/CODING_PLAN.md, using official pages where possible. The paid-site pricing research from October 25, 2026 is on the `paid-site` branch.

## Leading K-8 coding offerings
| Offering | Grades | Languages | Strengths | Gaps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Code.org CS Fundamentals | K–5 | Blocks | Free, strong lesson plans and teacher support, a separate AI course for grades 3–5 | Stays in blocks; little career or local context |
| Code.org CS Discoveries (becoming AI Discoveries, 2026–27) | 6–10 | Game Lab, App Lab, Web Lab, AI Lab | Units on web, games, data, physical computing, and AI | No Python; workshops cost about $1,000 |
| CodeHS (middle school) | 6–8 | Karel, Python (Tracy the Turtle), JavaScript | The best ramp from blocks to text; autograding | Paid; generic projects |
| Tynker | about 7–14 | Blocks to Python/JS | Engaging, Minecraft modding | Paid; game-like, little real data |
| CodeCombat / Ozaria | 3–12 | Python/JS from day one | Real text code inside a story game | Paid for full access; no real-world context |
| Scratch / ScratchEd Creative Computing | all | Blocks | Free, creative, "Debug-It" studios | Little assessment; stays in blocks |
| Google CS First | — | — | Shut down on June 30, 2025; points to Raspberry Pi's Experience CS (free, Scratch, ages 8–14, beta) | — |
| Bootstrap (Brown) | 4–12 | Pyret/Python, data science | Real datasets, math-aligned | Training about $1,500 per teacher; academic feel |
| MakeCode (micro:bit, Arcade) | 4–12 | Blocks / JS / Python | Real retro games, hardware | Needs hardware for micro:bit; no career or AI framing |
| Khan Academy computing | 6–12 | JavaScript, Python | Free, autograded | Not grade-banded; lecture-heavy |

**Gaps that keep showing up:**
- local, real-world data;
- links from lessons to careers;
- a gradual route from blocks to text;
- **critiquing AI-written code** (a 2025 study found that children using AI coding tools test but rarely read the code);
- accessibility and security basics;
- students explaining their own work.

## Skills employers and frameworks emphasize (2025–26)
- **CSTA:** the standards revision (Draft 3.0) weaves AI and cybersecurity throughout. As of October 2026, the final release isn't confirmed.
- **AI4K12:** the Five Big Ideas (Perception, Representation & Reasoning, Learning, Natural Interaction, Societal Impact).
- **OECD/EC AILit draft:** Engaging with, Creating with, Managing, and Designing AI.
- **ISTE Computational Thinking competencies** and the **NICE K-12 cybersecurity** roadmap.
- **Concrete skills:**
  - decomposition;
  - predicting and tracing code;
  - reading and changing others' code;
  - testing and debugging;
  - version history and collaboration;
  - working with data;
  - judging AI output and fixing AI-written code;
  - accessibility;
  - passwords, phishing, and privacy;
  - explaining technical work.

## Maine
- **Standards:** no single statewide CS standard; districts choose. LD 1382 required K–12 CS standards and a state plan. Maine has no CS teaching endorsement.
- **MMSA** "STEM Workforce Ready": $8.2M (Harold Alfond Foundation) to train about 1,000 educators and reach 20,000 rural pre-K–8 students in computational thinking.
- **Educate Maine / Project>Login:** Girls Who Code camps, and Maine's Code.org regional partner (with MMSA).
- **Maine App Challenge** (Tyler Technologies, UMaine, Educate Maine): ages 13+, with scholarships and internship interviews.
- **GMRI LabVenture:** about 10,000 5th–6th graders a year, working with sea-temperature and fishery data.
- **Blue economy:** about 88,700 jobs (Maine DECD, 2025).
- **Industries with tech roles:** biotech and health, finance and payments, shipbuilding, ocean technology. STEM 207 names roles and industries only, never a company without permission.

## What works with 10–14 year olds
- **Interests:** game making and mods (Roblox and Minecraft dominate ages 8–12), AI for fun (86% of ages 9–17 have used it), robotics, music and code, apps for local problems.
- **Methods:**
  - **PRIMM** (Predict, Run, Investigate, Modify, Make; Raspberry Pi Foundation);
  - **Parsons problems** (put scrambled code in order; they help students with low confidence);
  - use-modify-create;
  - pair programming;
  - project-based and culturally responsive computing.
